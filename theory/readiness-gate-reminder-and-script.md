# Readiness Gate: Reminder and Workshop Script

The short version for running the workshop. The full guide (21 sections, examples, evals) lives in its own repo: [otmanm/readiness-gate](https://github.com/otmanm/readiness-gate/blob/main/readiness-gate-for-non-technical-operators.md).

## One-Page Reminder

Use this section before any AI-generated plan, roadmap, website build, coding plan, business launch, outreach campaign, hiring plan, fitness plan, nutrition plan, or agent workflow.

### The One Rule

No normal plan first. Run the Readiness Gate first.

### The Fast Checklist

1. **Decision:** What decision must this plan support?
2. **Context:** What do we know, and what is missing?
3. **Resources:** What time, money, people, tools, data, assets, skills, access, and relationships are actually available?
4. **Field truth:** What has been checked with real people, real users, real buyers, logs, tests, operations, field visits, or expert review?
5. **Preparation:** What must exist before execution starts?
6. **Minimum viable preparedness:** What is the minimum preparation that reduces the major risks without creating research theater?
7. **Options:** What cheap Plan B, Plan C, or Plan D should we prepare so we are not trapped by one fragile plan?
8. **Red team:** If this plan failed, what probably caused the failure?
9. **Smallest reality test:** What is the smallest action that creates real evidence?
10. **Gate:** What evidence means continue, pivot, pause, or kill?
11. **Stop loss:** When do we stop spending time, money, attention, reputation, or energy?
12. **Reinvestment:** What artifact, template, data, contact, skill, workflow, or proof should be saved so the next loop gets faster?

### Readiness Levels

| Level | Meaning | Correct output |
|---|---|---|
| Red | Goal, resources, or field truth are missing. | Discovery or context packet. |
| Orange | Some ingredients exist, but execution would rely on guessing. | Preparation plan. |
| Yellow | Enough exists for one controlled test, not a full rollout. | Smallest reality test. |
| Green | Major requirements, evidence, resources, and gates are clear. | Execution plan with checkpoints. |

### The Sentence to Remember

A plan without context, resources, field truth, options, red teaming, metrics, and stop loss is not a plan. It is AI theater.


## Workshop Facilitation Script

From section 17 of the full guide.

If you teach this method in a workshop, do not begin with theory. Begin with a bad plan.

Put a generic AI plan on the screen. For example:

"Week 1: define the offer. Week 2: build the website. Week 3: launch outreach. Week 4: close clients."

Then ask the room:

- What did this plan assume?
- What resources did it invent?
- What field truth is missing?
- What could block Week 1?
- What could make Week 2 pointless?
- What would make Week 3 damage reputation?
- What would make Week 4 impossible?

The point of the workshop is to make people feel the difference between a plan that is grammatically correct and a plan that is operationally real.

Then run the same exercise with fitness:

"Train five days per week. Eat 180 grams of protein. Sleep eight hours."

Ask:

- What if the person works nights?
- What if they have knee pain?
- What if they do not cook?
- What if they travel twice per month?
- What if they hate the foods in the plan?
- What if they miss Monday and mentally quit the week?

This helps non-technical people understand that the problem is not AI, coding, or business jargon. The problem is that any serious plan must fit reality.

After that, introduce the Readiness Gate as a professional habit:

1. Intake before plan.
2. Evidence before confidence.
3. Preparation before execution.
4. Options before panic.
5. Small test before scale.
6. Stop loss before sunk cost.
7. Reinvestment before forgetting.

The best workshop exercise is to split a group into two teams.

Team A asks AI for a normal plan.

Team B runs the Readiness Gate first.

Compare the outputs. Team A will usually have a cleaner calendar. Team B will usually have a messier but more truthful operating picture. This is the lesson. Early truth looks messy. Late truth becomes expensive.
